Tumor Classification Guide

NeuroScan classifies three primary intracranial neoplasm categories — selected for their clinical prevalence and radiological distinctiveness on T1-weighted MRI. Each carries a fundamentally different prognosis and treatment pathway.

01
High Risk · Malignant
Glioma
Primary Malignant Brain Tumor
Arising from glial cells — astrocytes and oligodendrocytes — gliomas are the most common primary brain tumor. They span WHO Grades I–IV, from slow-growing pilocytic astrocytoma to the highly aggressive Glioblastoma Multiforme (GBM), which carries a median survival of ~15 months even with aggressive multimodal treatment.
Prevalence
~45% of primary brain tumors. Peak incidence ages 45–70.
WHO Grades
Grade I (pilocytic) to Grade IV (GBM) — grade defines aggressiveness.
Molecular Markers
IDH1/2, MGMT methylation, 1p/19q codeletion.
Symptoms
Seizures, headaches, cognitive decline, motor weakness.
Treatment
Maximal safe resection + Temozolomide + radiotherapy (Stupp protocol). Bevacizumab for recurrent GBM.
02
Moderate Risk · Usually Benign
Meningioma
Benign Meningeal Tumor
Originating from arachnoid cap cells of the meninges, meningiomas are the most prevalent primary brain tumor and typically slow-growing and benign (WHO Grade I). They disproportionately affect women aged 40–70 and are frequently discovered incidentally on neuroimaging with no symptoms.
Prevalence
~37% of primary brain tumors. Female-to-male ratio ~2:1.
Location
Brain surface, skull base, falx cerebri, optic nerve sheath.
Symptoms
Often asymptomatic; headaches, vision loss, focal seizures.
Recurrence
Grade I: ~10%. Grade III (anaplastic): 50–80%.
Treatment
Observation (small lesions); surgical resection often curative; Gamma Knife radiosurgery for residual or recurrent tumor.
03
Moderate Risk · Benign Adenoma
Pituitary
Pituitary Gland Adenoma
Pituitary adenomas arise from secretory cells of the anterior pituitary at the base of the brain. Most are benign but cause significant morbidity through hormonal dysregulation or mass effect on the optic chiasm — producing the characteristic bitemporal visual field loss. Microadenomas (<10mm) are found incidentally in ~10% of autopsies.
Prevalence
~16% of primary brain tumors. Most common sellar region tumor.
Functional Types
Prolactinoma, GH-secreting (acromegaly), ACTH-secreting (Cushing's), non-functioning.
Symptoms
Bitemporal hemianopia, hormonal imbalance, headaches, fatigue.
Diagnosis
MRI + hormonal blood panel. Dynamic testing for Cushing's.
Treatment
Dopamine agonists (cabergoline) for prolactinoma; transsphenoidal surgery; stereotactic radiosurgery for residual.
About
NeuroScan is an end-to-end diagnostic intelligence platform designed to automate the detection and classification of primary brain pathologies. By leveraging EfficientNetB0-based transfer learning, the system transforms raw MRI data into clinical insights, achieving a 97.02% Validation Accuracy and a 93.48% Test Accuracy across four distinct classifications: Glioma, Meningioma, Pituitary, and No Tumor.
01
Transfer Learning
Built on EfficientNetB0 pretrained on ImageNet. The frozen backbone extracts powerful visual features while a custom classifier head learns tumor-specific patterns from MRI data — achieving strong results with minimal training data.
02
Dataset
Trained on 13,305 MRI brain scans across Glioma, Meningioma, No Tumor, and Pituitary classes from the Masoud Nickparvar Kaggle Brain Tumor MRI Dataset with an 80/20 train-validation split.
03
Tech Stack
Python · TensorFlow · Keras · Flask · Groq LLaMA. Trained with heavy data augmentation, early stopping, and ReduceLROnPlateau. Frontend served as a REST API with a single-page vanilla JS interface.
Model Scope & Limitations — NeuroScan classifies four categories only: Glioma, Meningioma, Pituitary Adenoma, and No Tumor. Other primary tumors (Medulloblastoma, Ependymoma, Craniopharyngioma, CNS Lymphoma) and brain metastases are outside training scope and may be misclassified with high confidence — a known limitation of all closed-set classification models. Always confirm AI-generated results with a qualified radiologist.
